Cookie banners

Strict Switch can click "reject" on cookie banners for you, then check whether the site recorded your rejection. It's off until you switch it on, because it needs permission to read every site you visit.

Off

Switching this on asks your browser to let Strict Switch "read and change all your data on all websites". That's what finding and answering cookie banners takes. It all happens on your computer; nothing is sent anywhere.

Access is handed back by itself after 90 days unless you renew it, and you'll be reminded every 7 days that it's on.

On

Sorry about the odd spot for this. Your reminder and the date access ends are kept on this page, apart from the rest of Strict Switch's settings, so nothing running inside web pages can quietly postpone them.

Why is this stored here?

How auto-reject works. While it's on, Strict Switch adds a small script to every page you open. The script looks for a cookie banner. If it recognises one, using DuckDuckGo's open-source autoconsent rules (bundled with Strict Switch, never downloaded), it clicks the option to reject. It never clicks "accept". Strict Switch then reads the site's consent record (IAB TCF and Google Consent Mode, which most banners use) to check that the rejection registered, and writes what happened in the activity log.

Why page scripts are kept away from this setting. Scripts that run inside web pages can reach most of an extension's stored settings, and a misbehaving page could try to use them. So your reminder and the date access ends are kept in a separate store that only Strict Switch's own pages can reach, and they only change when you click a button on this page. They're stored as dates. Anything Strict Switch can't read, or anything further away than allowed (60 days for the reminder, 90 days for access), counts as "now". So interference can only make the reminder come sooner, or access end sooner, never later.

If reminders keep coming sooner than you chose, something may be interfering with Strict Switch, such as another extension or software on your computer. Check where your copy came from (below), and consider turning access off.
